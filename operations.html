Operational Reconciliation

Your system of record is only as good as what's in it.

Messy inputs arrive every day: invoices, photos, documents, emails. When someone checks them manually, it takes hours and things get missed. We automate the check, flag what doesn't match, and route exceptions to the right person — before a missed line item or wrong record becomes a real problem.

The pattern

The same underlying problem across every operation

Most operational accuracy problems share a structure: something comes in from the outside world, and someone has to manually verify it against what a system already knows. An invoice against a property record. A vendor photo against a work order. A document against a checklist of required fields. A record against what was expected to be filed by a deadline.

The check itself isn't complicated. It's just tedious, time-consuming, and easy to miss when the volume is high. That's exactly the kind of work AI automation handles well.

We build the automation that does the check automatically: reads the input, compares it against your system of record, identifies what matches and what doesn't, and either resolves it or routes the exception to a human. What used to take an afternoon of cross-referencing takes seconds, and nothing slips through because someone was busy.

How it works in plain terms Something arrives (an invoice, a document, a photo, a submission). The automation reads it, checks it against what your system says should be there, and flags anything that's off. If it matches, it logs and moves on. If it doesn't, it routes the exception to the right person with the context they need to fix it.
What this looks like in practice

Three workflows we build repeatedly

These aren't the only versions. They're the patterns that come up most often across the teams we work with.

Document review against a system of record

Documents arrive: reports, submissions, completed forms. The automation reads each one and checks it against what your system says should be there. Missing required fields, wrong values, and inconsistencies surface immediately instead of during a review weeks later.

Common inputs: PDFs, forms, structured reports. Common systems: databases, CRMs, property records, client files.

Vendor and invoice validation

Invoices arrive from vendors. The automation matches line items against expected costs and your internal records using whatever key makes sense for your operation: address, project ID, account number. It surfaces what matches, what doesn't, and what's missing, before anything gets approved.

Common inputs: invoices, receipts, vendor reports. Common systems: accounting software, property databases, project management tools.

Pre-deadline gap detection

Before a submission deadline, review cycle, or audit window, the automation scans your records and flags what's incomplete, missing, or past due. Your team spends their time fixing the gaps, not finding them. The exceptions land in front of the right person with enough lead time to act.

Common inputs: internal records, status logs, required document lists. Common systems: CRMs, compliance trackers, project databases.

How we build it

Three steps to a running reconciliation automation

1

Map the input and the record

We start by understanding what's coming in, what your system of record contains, and what a match looks like versus a discrepancy. This shapes everything: what the automation reads, what it checks against, and what counts as an exception worth routing.

2
