We build the automation that does the check: reads the input, compares it against your system of record, resolves what matches, and routes the rest to a human with the context they need to act. What used to take an afternoon takes seconds, and nothing slips through because someone was busy.

How it works in plain terms Something arrives (an invoice, a document, a photo). The automation reads it, checks it against what your system says should be there, and flags anything that's off. If it matches, it logs and moves on. If it doesn't, it routes the exception to the right person with enough context to fix it.
What this looks like in practice

Three workflows we build repeatedly

These aren't the only versions. They're the patterns that come up most often across the teams we work with.

Document review against a system of record

Documents arrive: reports, submissions, completed forms. The automation reads each one and checks it against what your system says should be there. Missing required fields, wrong values, and inconsistencies surface immediately instead of during a review weeks later.

Common inputs: PDFs, forms, structured reports. Common systems: databases, CRMs, property records, client files.

Vendor and invoice validation

Invoices arrive from vendors. The automation matches line items against expected costs and your internal records using whatever key makes sense for your operation: address, project ID, account number. It surfaces what matches, what doesn't, and what's missing, before anything gets approved.

Common inputs: invoices, receipts, vendor reports. Common systems: accounting software, property databases, project management tools.

Pre-deadline gap detection

Before a submission deadline, review cycle, or audit window, the automation scans your records and flags what's incomplete, missing, or past due. Your team spends their time fixing the gaps, not finding them. The exceptions land in front of the right person with enough lead time to act.

Common inputs: internal records, status logs, required document lists. Common systems: CRMs, compliance trackers, project databases.
