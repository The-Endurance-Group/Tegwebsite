Claude Haiku 5.5 cuts token prices up to 90%. Here is what to move over first.

Conor Sullivan · Vice President · October 8, 2026
AI Automations

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on October 7, and the headline is the price: $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens, down from $1.00 and $5.00 on Haiku 4.5. That is a 90% cut on paper. Here is what it means in practice for teams running Claude in their operations, and where the savings are real.

The new pricing

Per published list prices, Haiku 5.5 has two tiers based on prompt size. Batch API requests get another 50% off.

  • Prompts up to 100K tokens: $0.10 input, $0.50 output, $0.01 cache reads.
  • Prompts over 100K tokens: $0.50 input, $2.50 output, $0.05 cache reads.
  • Haiku 4.5 for comparison: $1.00 input, $5.00 output, $0.10 cache reads, at any size.

The model also has a 1M token context window and up to 128K tokens of output. Anthropic estimates that real workloads come out around 75% cheaper than Haiku 4.5 once request sizes and token usage are accounted for, which is a more honest number than 90%.

The catch: the 100K line and the new tokenizer

Haiku 5.5 uses the same tokenizer as Claude 4.7 and later. According to the migration guide summary, the same text produces roughly 30% more tokens than it did on Haiku 4.5. The price tier is counted in the new tokens, so a prompt that was 90K tokens before is about 117K now and lands in the higher tier.

The same source works through two examples. A 60K-token turn gets about 87% cheaper. A 100K-token turn gets about 35% cheaper. Most requests will sit comfortably under the line (Anthropic says 90% of Haiku 4.5 requests were under 100K), but if you stuff whole documents or long chat histories into every call, count your tokens before you promise anyone a 90% saving.

Does it still do the work?

On Anthropic's own benchmarks, Haiku 5.5 is a big jump over Haiku 4.5. DataCamp reports 72.4% on OSWorld 2.1 versus 15.7% for Haiku 4.5, and 45.9% on Humanity's Last Exam without tools versus 10.2%. It also beats OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna on every benchmark where both are reported. Sonnet 5.5 still leads on all of them, and these are vendor-reported numbers, not independent tests.

The most useful real-world data point is DataCamp's small accounts payable test: 24 invoices, each needing a pay, hold, or return decision. Both Haiku models got all 24 right. Haiku 5.5 cost $0.0098 and Haiku 4.5 cost $0.25, about 25 times more, partly because Haiku 4.5 burned a large thinking budget. It is one small test, but it matches what we would expect from a model built for this kind of job.

What we would move to Haiku 5.5 first

The sweet spot is high-volume work with a clear right answer and a short prompt:

  • Classification and routing: tagging inbound leads, sorting support tickets, labeling emails.
  • Extraction: pulling fields from invoices, contracts, forms, and job postings into your CRM or a spreadsheet.
  • First-pass summaries: call notes, long threads, meeting transcripts that a person or a stronger model reviews next.
  • Subagent reading: a cheap Haiku step that reads and condenses material for a Sonnet or Opus step that makes the decision.

Keep Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5 for anything that needs judgment, long multi-step reasoning, or code. Anthropic itself says those two remain the better choices for complex agentic coding. If you are not sure where your workflows fall, our guide to choosing between Claude models covers the tradeoffs.

What to check before you switch

If your automations call the API directly, the swap is not just a new model name. The ID is claude-haiku-5-5, and several things that worked on Haiku 4.5 now return errors, including manual thinking budgets, non-default temperature settings, and prefilled assistant turns. Responses can also begin with a thinking block, so code that reads the first content block by position needs to change. If your workflows run inside Claude's own apps, none of this applies to you.

Our approach is simple: pick one high-volume workflow, run it on both models against a sample of real inputs, compare accuracy and cost, and only then switch. Frequently asked questions

How much cheaper is Claude Haiku 5.5 than Haiku 4.5?

For prompts up to 100K tokens, input drops from $1.00 to $0.10 per million tokens and output from $5.00 to $0.50, a 90% cut. Above 100K tokens the rates are $0.50 and $2.50, a 50% cut. Anthropic estimates real workloads cost about 75% less once the new tokenizer is counted.

What is the catch with the 100K token threshold?

Haiku 5.5 uses a newer tokenizer that produces roughly 30% more tokens for the same text. A prompt that was 90K tokens on Haiku 4.5 is about 117K on Haiku 5.5, which puts it in the higher price tier. Count tokens on your real prompts before assuming the full 90% savings.

Should we replace Sonnet 5.5 with Haiku 5.5?

Not across the board. Haiku 5.5 is a strong fit for high-volume, well-defined work like classification, extraction, routing and summarization. Sonnet 5.5 still leads on every benchmark Anthropic published and remains the better choice for complex agentic work.

Do we need to change our existing Claude workflows to use it?

If your automations call the API directly, yes. The model ID is claude-haiku-5-5, and several parameters that worked on Haiku 4.5, such as manual thinking budgets and non-default sampling settings, now return errors. Workflows inside Claude's own apps are not affected.
