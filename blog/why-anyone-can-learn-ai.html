Why I Believe Anyone Can Learn AI (Even a Former English Teacher)

Conor Sullivan · Vice President · September 11, 2026
Professional Development

A few years ago, I was grading essays and planning lesson plans for high school English classes. This week, I earned my Claude Certified Developer credential at the Foundations level. If you'd told 28-year-old me that was coming, I wouldn't have believed you.

I bring this up because I think a lot of business owners talk themselves out of AI for the same reason I would have: they assume it requires a technical background they don't have.

The skills that actually transfer

Teaching high school English turned out to be surprisingly good preparation for this work. Every day I had to take something complicated and make it click for someone seeing it for the first time. I had to read a room and adjust mid-lesson when something wasn't landing. I had to explain the "why" behind a rule, not just hand someone the rule.

That's most of what building good AI systems for a business actually requires. Understanding how a team really works, where the friction is, and translating that into something a system can do. The technical side is learnable. Understanding people and processes is the harder skill, and it's the one I already had.

What this means if you're on the fence

If you've been putting off exploring AI for your business because it feels like it's "for the technical people," here's the honest version: it isn't. The businesses getting the most out of AI right now aren't the ones with the most technical staff. They're the ones willing to have a clear conversation about what's actually slowing them down — whether that's follow-up emails piling up, leads going cold, or reporting that eats an afternoon every week.

We built The Endurance Group around that idea. We're not asking clients to learn how the systems work under the hood. We're asking them what's broken, and then we build the fix.

Still learning, still building

Foundations level is one step. I'll keep building on it, and I'll keep sharing what that looks like here — both the technical wins and the client-facing lessons.

If you're curious what AI could realistically do inside your business, or you want to swap stories about unlikely career pivots, let's talk.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a technical background to learn AI and build AI systems for business?

No. The skills that matter most in building useful AI systems for a business are understanding how a team works, where the friction is, and being able to translate that clearly into something a system can act on. The technical side is learnable. The harder skills — reading a room, explaining a process, identifying what's actually slowing things down — come from experience in communication-heavy roles, not engineering.

What is the Claude Certified Developer credential?

The Claude Certified Developer credential is an official certification from Anthropic for professionals who build systems and automations using the Claude API. The Foundations level covers core concepts for working with Claude effectively — prompting, evaluation, and building reliable automations. It's designed for practitioners building real AI tools, not just experimenters.

Can someone without a coding background use AI to help their business?

Yes. Business owners don't need to build AI systems themselves — they need to be able to describe what's slowing their team down clearly enough for someone else to build the fix. The businesses getting the most out of AI right now aren't necessarily the most technical ones. They're the ones willing to have an honest conversation about what's broken, whether that's follow-up emails piling up, leads going cold, or reporting that eats an afternoon every week.
