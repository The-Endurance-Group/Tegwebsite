Building a Tax Advisory AI Where Accuracy Comes First

How The Endurance Group built a tax advisory AI for Benchmark Human Capital, and why accuracy came before every other feature.

The Client

Benchmark Human Capital is a professional employer organization (PEO) that provides HR services for small businesses. Its Benchmark IQ brand covers tax advisory and strategy, financial advisory and CFO services, and accounting. Michael Gazzano is the CEO.

The Challenge

In tax, an answer that sounds right and is wrong is worse than no answer. Benchmark wanted an AI that tax professionals could use as a reference, not one that improvised.

The firm already had the raw material: a library of tax strategies and a tax resolution guide. The question was how to turn that expertise into a tool people could trust, and then into something Benchmark could offer to customers.

The Approach

TEG measured before building. The engagement began on May 31, 2024, with a side-by-side comparison. The same tax scenarios went through competing tools, and TEG compared the answers and the IRS sources each one cited. The scenarios were realistic ones: a document checklist for a couple with two W-2 jobs, a side business, stock sales and a college student with work-study income; a move from sole proprietorship to LLC; an international expansion; and a home office deduction course outline.

In September 2024, after a call with Gazzano, the next phase was set. Replace the sample plan documents in the AI's knowledge base with Benchmark's own tax strategies. Put those documents behind a login so new users have to sign up. Map a path from Facebook ads to sign-ups. TEG planned four weeks to implement, then tuning through October 31.

The Solution

TEG built the tax advisory AI around a few decisions:

  • A tax planner persona that builds a taxpayer profile one question at a time: filing status, income sources, business ownership, real estate, retirement, deductions and life events, skipping what does not apply
  • Instructions to reference the US tax code directly and link to online resources
  • A second persona for tax resolution, tested with questions about IRS notices such as the CP14 and CP523 and what to expect in an audit
  • A knowledge base of Benchmark's own tax strategies, from accountable plans and Section 179 to cost segregation, the QBI deduction and the R&D credit
  • Account creation and login on the Benchmark IQ website, with order confirmation and cancellation emails

How Accuracy Was Handled

Accuracy was the requirement every other feature had to serve. In practice that meant three things. The AI was told to work from the tax code and link to sources. It was fed Benchmark's own strategies rather than sample documents. And it was compared with other tools on the same scenarios, answer by answer and source by source. It is a reference for professionals and does not replace a CPA's judgment.

The Result

The engagement ran from May 31 to December 18, 2024. It ended with a working tax AI, a login-gated website to offer it from and, in Gazzano's words, "a clear strategy for growth." His full statement:

"The Endurance Group was instrumental in helping me create an AI that knows the US tax code inside and out, providing an invaluable reference tool for tax professionals. They also offered insightful business advice on how to scale our AI solution effectively. Thanks to TEG, we now have a powerful tool that enhances our service offerings and a clear strategy for growth. I highly recommend The Endurance Group for their exceptional technical and business acumen."

Michael Gazzano, CEO, Benchmark Human Capital

Frequently asked questions

What is the Benchmark IQ tax advisory AI?

It is a tax advisory AI that The Endurance Group built with Benchmark Human Capital for tax professionals. It has a tax planning persona and a tax resolution persona, is instructed to reference the US tax code directly and link to online resources, and draws on Benchmark's own tax strategies.

How was the tax AI tested for accuracy?

TEG ran the same realistic tax scenarios through Benchmark's AI and competing tools and compared the answers and the IRS sources each one cited. TEG is not publishing an accuracy percentage because it does not have a verified one.
