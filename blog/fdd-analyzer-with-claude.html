Why FDDs are hard to use as a lead source

A Franchise Disclosure Document is long, dense, and formatted for lawyers, not for sales teams. The most useful part for outreach is the section listing current franchisees, which typically includes names and contact details. Finding it, reading it, and copying it into a spreadsheet by hand is slow, and it's easy to miss entries or make copying errors.

What FDD Analyzer does

  1. Reads the whole document. You give Claude the FDD and it works through the full text, not just the first pages.
  2. Finds the franchisee listings. It locates the sections that list current franchise owners.
  3. Extracts names and contact details. Each owner becomes one row with the details the document provides.
  4. Returns a structured list. The output is a clean table you can drop into a spreadsheet or your CRM for outreach.

The version we built for clients extracts every franchise owner contact from a 200-page FDD in under three minutes.

Who uses it

  • Franchisors and franchise development teams researching the market and their competitors.
  • Franchise brokers and consultants building prospect and contact lists.

Use the list carefully

The skill only pulls what the document already publishes. Before you contact anyone, check the FTC Franchise Rule, any state franchise laws that apply, and your own policies on outreach. Spot-check a handful of rows against the source document before you rely on the list.
