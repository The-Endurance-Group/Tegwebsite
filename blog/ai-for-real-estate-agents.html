AI for real estate agents: what to automate first

Conor Sullivan · Vice President · November 4, 2026 · Last updated November 4, 2026
Real Estate

AI for real estate agents works best on repeatable work: listing and transaction checklists, listing marketing, document follow-up and client follow-up. Start with one checklist you already run, turn it into an assistant that drafts and tracks each step, and keep every client-facing message in your approval. That order saves time fast without putting your reputation at risk.

The Endurance Group (TEG) is a Claude Implementation Partner and an official member of the Anthropic Claude Partner Network. We build Claude Skills, Projects and integrations around the way real people already work, including individual agents and brokers.

Key takeaways

  • Automate the checklist you already follow before you try anything new.
  • Let AI draft and track. You approve anything a client, a broker or the MLS will see.
  • Your own SOPs are the best instructions you can give an AI, so turn each one into a skill instead of writing prompts from scratch.
  • Keep the AI tool you already use if it holds months of your history.
  • Build on accounts you own, so the setup moves with you if you change brokerages.

What can AI do for a real estate agent?

AI can draft, track and organize the repeatable parts of an agent's week. That covers listing and buyer checklists, just-listed e-blasts, listing descriptions, showing and open house follow-up, signed-document chasing, file and photo organization, and a daily summary of what needs attention.

Each of these is work an agent or an assistant already does by hand. AI does not invent new work. It takes steps off your plate:

  • Checklists for every deal. A seller or buyer checklist is created when a deal starts and checked off as work is done.
  • Listing marketing drafts. E-blasts, listing descriptions and broker outreach, written in your voice for your approval.
  • Document follow-up. Signed documents are requested, tracked and verified, with a nudge drafted when something is late.
  • Files and photos. Property folders and listing media are filed and named the same way every time.
  • A morning brief. One message listing what needs you today, what is drafted and what is waiting on someone else.

What should a real estate agent automate first?

With AI for real estate agents, the best first project is the checklist you already follow for each listing or buyer. The steps are repeatable, you already know what good looks like, and every step you automate saves time on every future deal.

Starting with a chatbot or a new lead source is the common mistake. Those projects depend on things you have not built yet. A checklist depends only on steps you already take. Here is how the common starting points compare:

Where to start with AI as an agent
TaskRiskYour role
Listing and buyer checklistsLowGlance at the list each morning
Listing marketing draftsLow to mediumReview for accuracy and Fair Housing language
Document follow-upLowApprove the nudges
Client follow-up messagesMediumApprove every message before it sends
Pricing and negotiation adviceHighKeep it yours

How do you turn your SOPs into an AI assistant?

You turn each written procedure into a skill, which is a saved set of instructions the AI follows every time. The process has six steps:

  1. List the steps in order. From a signed agreement through closing, a rough list is enough.
  2. Attach a procedure to each step. Note where the file lives, which template to use and who gets the email.
  3. Turn each procedure into a skill. Point the skill at your own document, so when you update the document the assistant follows without retraining. Anthropic describes Skills as folders of instructions the AI loads when a task calls for them.
  4. Connect the tools each step touches. Email, calendar, notes, cloud storage and design templates are the usual ones.
  5. Run it on real deals for a week. Write down anything that feels off.
  6. Refine it. Fix the steps that missed and add the ones you thought of while using it.

What does an AI assistant look like day to day?

An agent types "agreement signed, 12 Palm Ave" and a note for that property appears with the full seller checklist: photography booked, photos filed, just-listed e-blast drafted for approval, MLS entry and open house, and signed documents verified. Items check off as work is completed. Each morning a brief lists what needs the agent, what is drafted and what is waiting on others.

TEG is building this now for a Florida-based agent who specializes in luxury, starting from the agent's own written SOPs. There are 18 of them, covering listing marketing, transaction coordination, closing prep and expense tracking. The agent chose to stay on ChatGPT, because six months of saved history made switching a poor trade.

Should real estate agents use ChatGPT or Claude?

Either can work. The better choice is usually the one you already use, because your saved history and habits live there. TEG builds on Claude or ChatGPT, whichever the agent already uses.

  • You have months of ChatGPT history: stay on ChatGPT and build the assistant around it.
  • Your team or brokerage already standardizes on one tool: use that one.
  • You are starting fresh: compare the connectors each tool offers for your email, calendar and storage, then pick. Our guide to Claude vs ChatGPT for business automation covers what to weigh.

What should stay human?

Pricing advice, negotiation and anything fiduciary should stay with you. AI drafts, and you decide.

  • Pricing and valuation advice. Use AI for research and drafts, not for the call itself.
  • Negotiation and client counsel. This is the work clients hire you for.
  • Client-facing messages. The AI drafts, and you send.
  • Compliance. Review marketing against the federal Fair Housing Act, the NAR Code of Ethics and your brokerage's policies.

What if my brokerage's platforms block AI?

Build around them. Many brokerage-owned systems, such as the MLS, transaction management and internal CRMs, do not let outside AI connect. The assistant tracks tasks and drafts in tools you own, like your email, notes and cloud storage, and you enter the final step in the brokerage system yourself.

There is an upside. Because the assistant lives in your own accounts, it comes with you if you change brokerages.

Is it safe to give AI access to my email and files?

It can be, with limits you control. Set the assistant to draft only, so nothing sends without your click. Connect only the accounts you choose, and check your brokerage's policy before connecting anything that holds client data. You can revoke access at any time in the settings of the account you connected.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI write real estate listing descriptions?

Yes, as a first draft in your voice. You then review it for accuracy and Fair Housing language before it goes to the MLS or a client. The AI saves the blank-page time. You stay responsible for what gets published.

Will AI replace my assistant or transaction coordinator?

No. AI handles routine tracking and drafting, such as checklists, reminders and first-draft emails. Your assistant or coordinator keeps the judgment, the vendor relationships and the client calls, and usually gets more done with the routine work off their desk.

How long does it take to set up an AI assistant for an agent?

With TEG, it takes three calls of about an hour each, with the build happening between calls and a week of real use before the final refinement call. Your own time is roughly three hours in total.

Do I need to be technical to use AI as an agent?

No. You ask for things in everyday words, such as "agreement signed, 12 Palm Ave." The setup is done for you, and each step is explained in plain language when it is installed.

Does this work for buyer's agents too?

Yes. The same approach gives each buyer a checklist from first showing to closing, drafts showing follow-ups and new-match emails, and tracks the documents each deal needs.
