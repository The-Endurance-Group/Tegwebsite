What to automate first when you're a small B2B team

Jim Sullivan · President · July 3, 2026
AI Automations

The most common first question when a team gets serious about automation is: where do we start? The technology isn't the problem. Picking the right place to use it is.

Why "automate everything" is the wrong starting point

When teams get excited about automation, the list of things that could be automated gets long fast, CRM updates, outreach, onboarding, reporting, follow-up. The problem is that five half-built automations don't help anyone. Scope creep kills automation projects before they produce anything useful.

One working automation that runs reliably every day beats a whole roadmap that never gets deployed. The goal of the first build is to prove the value, not cover the whole process map.

Start with the thing that's breaking right now

The right first automation isn't the one that sounds impressive in a strategy session, it's the one causing visible pain today. Leads falling through the cracks. CRM fields that nobody fills in. Follow-up that gets skipped because something else came up. These are the tasks your team is already working around or complaining about.

Fix one of those first. You'll have proof of value that makes the second automation easy to justify, and a team that actually wants the next one.

The three categories that move the needle fastest

After working with B2B companies across real estate, technology, and professional services, three categories come up in nearly every conversation. First: inbound lead response time. If you're not following up within the first few minutes of a form fill, you're losing deals to whoever responds faster. Second: repetitive data entry and CRM updates, the task most reps hate and most skip. Third: follow-up that falls through the cracks, the sequence of touches that should be happening but doesn't, because it depends on someone remembering to do it.

Any one of these is a strong candidate for a first automation. All three are fixable without a massive build.

How the first automation usually leads to the second

When one automation is working and your team can see it running, the conversation changes. Instead of skepticism, you start hearing "could we do the same thing for X?" Momentum matters as much as the technology. Starting with something that actually runs, not a prototype, not a demo, is what creates it.

That's why we build the first automation for free. Not as a teaser, but because seeing one work in your own systems is the fastest way to know whether to keep going.

Frequently asked questions

How do we know if something is worth automating?

If someone does it more than a few times a week, it's the same process each time, and it doesn't require reading the situation, it's probably automatable. If it requires judgment, it's not.

What if we don't know what's slowing us down?

That's a strategy conversation before it's an automation conversation. We do a session with you to map the work and find the bottlenecks. Usually takes an hour.

How long until we see results from the first automation?

Most of the automations we build have a noticeable impact within the first week they're live, not "ROI in Q4" but actually measurably faster or less manual within days.
