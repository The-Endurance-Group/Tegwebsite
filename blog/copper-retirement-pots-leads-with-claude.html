Why these notices are easy to misread

As carriers retire copper, businesses that still depend on analog lines, often for elevator phones, fire alarm and security communicators, and fax, need replacement paths. The notices that signal this are public, but they are dense. A copper facility retirement is not the same event as a Section 214 service discontinuance. A wire center name is not a town boundary. A proposed date is not a shutoff date. And some notices say outright that no current customers are affected. Outreach built on a misread notice damages trust fast.

What POTS Opportunity does

  • Market scan. Searches FCC public notices and carrier network disclosures for your states and time window, and returns a ranked table: carrier, notice type and ID, exact stated geography, filed and effective dates, stated customer impact, source, and confidence.
  • Notice check. Reads a specific filing, its exhibits, and any amendments, then labels it: verified affected customers, network change with unclear impact, no current customer impact stated, general carrier plan, superseded or withdrawn, or unverified. It ends with the exact question to ask the carrier or prospect.
  • Prospect research. For a verified notice area, finds publicly documented organizations that may rely on analog lines, such as multifloor buildings, hotels, healthcare sites, schools, and warehouses, with evidence links, an area-match rating, and a verification question for each.
  • Outreach. Careful emails and call openers that cite the notice in plain language and invite the recipient to verify their own services, plus a discovery checklist covering line inventory, devices, monitoring, backup power, and inspection needs.
  • Weekly brief. A cited report of new, revised, and withdrawn notices for your states, which you can set up as a recurring task in your Claude account.

Built not to overclaim

The plugin never tells a prospect their line is being shut off, that they are in an affected area, or that their elevator or alarm uses copper unless there's direct evidence. A business category is treated as a hypothesis, not a fact. Prospect lists are labeled as organizations to investigate, not confirmed POTS customers. When the evidence is weak, the outreach becomes an educational check-in instead of an urgent warning.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find copper retirement notices?

Carriers file them with the FCC and publish them on their network disclosure pages. The FCC's domestic Section 214 discontinuance page and carrier network event pages are good starting points. The free POTS Opportunity plugin has Claude search these sources and verify each notice.

Does a copper retirement notice mean POTS lines will stop working on that date?

Not by itself. A copper facility retirement notice, a Section 214 service discontinuance, and a customer-specific shutoff are different events with different dates. POTS Opportunity keeps them separate and labels what each notice does and does not establish.

Can POTS Opportunity tell me which businesses have POTS lines?

No tool can confirm that from public data alone. The plugin finds organizations in a verified notice area that may rely on analog lines, shows the evidence, and gives a verification question to ask each one.

Yes. The plugin and the 30-minute setup session are both free.
