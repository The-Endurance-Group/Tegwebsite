How Claude Automates the Work That Slows Your Team Down

Claude doesn't just answer questions. It executes multi-step workflows across your business systems — checking data, making decisions, taking action — on a schedule, without anyone pushing a button.

September 18, 2026 · The Endurance Group

Your team is doing work Claude should be doing

Most teams that use Claude use it the same way they use a search engine: ask a question, read the answer, go do the next thing. That's fine. But it's about 10% of what Claude can actually do.

The other 90% is automation — Claude working in the background, moving data, making decisions, and taking action across your business systems while your team focuses on work that actually requires them.

What a Claude workflow actually is

A Claude workflow is a multi-step sequence. Not a single prompt and response — a chain of actions Claude executes in order, often touching multiple systems along the way.

Here's a simple example: every morning, Claude checks your CRM for leads that came in overnight, scores each one based on criteria you defined, routes high-priority leads to the right rep with a draft outreach email already written, and logs everything back to the CRM. No one triggers it. No one moves the data. It runs.

That's one workflow. You can have dozens.

What makes multi-step workflows powerful

The value isn't any single step — it's the chain. Claude can:

  • Check — pull data from a source (CRM, inbox, spreadsheet, database)
  • Decide — apply logic you define (qualify this lead, flag this record, escalate this ticket)
  • Act — write a draft, send an alert, update a record, route to a person
  • Repeat — run on a schedule, on a trigger, or in response to an event

Each step depends on the one before it. That's what makes it a workflow rather than a one-off task.

Connectors: how Claude reaches your other tools

Claude doesn't work in a vacuum. It connects to your existing systems through MCP servers — purpose-built connections that give Claude read and write access to the tools your team already uses.

TEG builds MCP servers for the specific tools in your stack. That means Claude can:

  • Pull contact records from your CRM and push updates back
  • Read your inbox and draft replies or route messages
  • Post summaries or alerts to Slack channels
  • Read and write to Google Sheets, Notion, or your document tools
  • Connect to proprietary databases and legacy systems that standard integrations can't reach

The connector is what makes Claude part of your operation rather than a tool you visit separately.

What actually gets automated

The best candidates for automation are the tasks your team does repeatedly, on a schedule, with rules that don't change much. The work that's important enough to do right but not complex enough to need a person every time.

Common examples TEG builds:

  • Lead qualification — score and route inbound leads the moment they come in
  • Data reconciliation — compare records across systems and flag discrepancies
  • Alert generation — monitor for conditions and notify the right person
  • Follow-up scheduling — identify deals or relationships that need attention and surface them
  • Report assembly — pull data from multiple sources and produce a formatted summary
