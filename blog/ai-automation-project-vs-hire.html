Could Your Company Be the Alternative to a Hire?

Conor Sullivan · Vice President · October 6, 2026
Client Engagement

TEG is an official member of the Anthropic Claude Partner Network. We build Claude Skills, Projects, and MCP integrations for B2B ops teams. This post shares an internal workflow we built to find them. See our full Claude services.

Can a project replace an AI automation hire?

Often, yes. When a company posts a job for an AI automation role, it has already picked the problem, set aside budget and started looking for help. If the work can be defined as a handful of workflows, a scoped project can deliver it without a permanent hire.

When a company posts a job for an AI automation role, it is telling you exactly what it needs. So I asked a simple question: could my company be an option instead of a hire?

I run The Endurance Group, a Claude Implementation Partner. Most of our work starts with a business that already knows it wants AI to take something off its plate. A job posting is the same signal, written down and published for anyone to read. The company has picked the problem, set aside budget, and started looking for help.

So instead of waiting for those companies to find us, I built a workflow to find them.

How does the job posting workflow work?

The workflow runs every weekday morning and does the legwork a person would otherwise skip.

  1. Search. It searches Indeed and ZipRecruiter for postings that match what my firm does, such as AI automation specialist, workflow automation, and similar roles at US companies under 500 people.
  2. Filter. It filters out deep machine learning roles, companies that are already agencies, and anyone we have been in touch with before.
  3. Find the contact. For each company that fits, it uses Apollo to find the likely hiring manager, usually the owner or head of operations at a smaller company.
  4. Draft. It drafts an email and a LinkedIn message asking if they would be open to a short chat about a scoped project as an alternative to a full-time hire.

Nothing sends on its own. I read every draft and decide who actually hears from me.

Why is a job posting such a strong buying signal?

A job post is one of the clearest buying signals in business. Most outbound works backward, guessing who might need you. A posting works forward, because the company has already told you the problem and that it is willing to pay to solve it.

Three things stand out so far:

  • Contractor and fractional postings are the warmest. A company advertising for a consultant or part-time specialist has already accepted that outside help is fine. There is no need to talk them out of a full-time hire.
  • The ask is small. The message is an invitation to talk, not a pitch deck. People who are mid-search for a hire are usually happy to hear a second option.
  • Small companies post this work under many names. Titles like operations coordinator or marketing ops specialist often describe an automation project. Searching only for "AI" misses most of them.

We covered the original idea behind this approach in how we use hiring signals to find companies ready to buy AI. This version goes one step further by offering a different way to solve the problem the posting describes.

Should you hire for AI automation or run a project?

If you are the one hiring, answer this before you post the role: what are the three workflows you want automated?

If you can name them, you probably have a project, not a permanent position. Once those workflows are built, tested, and handed off, a full-time role may have little left to do.

If you can't name them, you have a strategy question, and that is a different conversation. Either way, spending a week on that exercise will make your job description, or your project scope, far sharper.

Sometimes the answer is still a hire, and that is fine. A company that knows what it wants built is in a much better position to hire well. For a related decision, see when to hire a sales rep and when to automate.

Frequently asked questions

Is a job posting for an AI automation role a good buying signal?

Yes. A company that posts an AI automation role has already chosen the problem, set aside budget and started looking for help. That makes it a stronger signal than a company list filtered by industry or headcount, which only guesses at need.

Can a project be a better fit than hiring a full-time AI automation specialist?

Often. If you can name the three workflows you want automated, you likely have a scoped project. Once those workflows are built, tested and handed off, a full-time role may have little left to do. If you cannot name them, you have a strategy question to answer first.

What job titles describe AI automation work at small companies?

Small companies post this work under many names, including operations coordinator, marketing ops specialist, workflow automation specialist and AI automation specialist. Searching only for the word AI misses most of these postings.

How does TEG find companies that are hiring for AI automation?

A daily workflow searches Indeed and ZipRecruiter for matching postings at US companies under 500 people, filters out machine learning roles, agencies and past contacts, then uses Apollo to find the likely hiring manager. It drafts an email and a LinkedIn message, and a person reviews every draft before anything is sent.
